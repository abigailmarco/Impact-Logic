The Vortex™ Helmet

The Vortex is a soft-shelled protective cooling helmet with a series of formulated gel capsules (55°F) designed to keep the brain cool. It helps reduce the chance of brain swelling in high heat and humidity, while also absorbing impact better than traditional foam helmets. Built for athletes especially in high heat and humidity.

Key Benefits

  • Gel capsules cool the brain and maintain 55–75°F for up to 45 minutes.
  • Absorbs impact more effectively than foam.
  • Reduces potential for brain swelling, concussive events, brain hyperthermia, and heat stroke.
  • Low-profile design is compatible with all current outer helmet brands and models.
  • Comes in 3 sizes. Liners are cleaned, disinfected, and recharged between uses.

How The Vortex Compares

Every helmet on the market is foam. Foam loses impact protection below 60°F and can bottom out above 100°F — and it locks in heat. The Vortex gel capsules absorb energy better than a foam helmet and actively cool the brain. No other helmet on the market does both.

The Science

After years of research, we found that heat inside a helmet can reach as high as 110°F — a contributing factor in traumatic brain injuries and possibly CTE. Our work connects the dots of peer-reviewed research from around the globe.

The Temperature Inside Football Helmets During Head Impact: A Five-Year Study of Collegiate Football Games

A five-year study of Virginia Tech football players measuring in-helmet temperatures across 60 games and 35,773 head impacts. Results showed temperatures ranging from 32.9°F to 106.2°F. This study was done in New Hampshire; average temperatures in Western and Southern states often exceed 110°F.

A Matter of Degrees: A Systematic Review of the Ergogenic Effect of Pre-Cooling in Highly Trained Athletes

A systematic review showing that pre-cooling methods are an effective strategy to increase sports performance in hot environments — supporting the science behind cooling the brain before and during athletic activity.

Head Pre-Cooling Improves 5-km Time-Trial Performance in Male Amateur Runners in the Heat

A study demonstrating that a 20-minute head-cooling intervention improved 5 km running performance in hot environments — suggesting head cooling is a practical and effective strategy for both professional and amateur athletes.
